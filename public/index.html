Warmup Manual
🌡

What is Email Warmup?

Email warmup is the process of building a positive sending reputation for a domain and email account before launching cold outreach campaigns. When a new email account sends a large volume of messages immediately, spam filters flag it as suspicious and deliverability collapses. Warmup fixes this by gradually simulating natural email activity — real sends, real replies, real engagement — so Gmail and other providers learn to trust your domain before you ever contact a prospect.

⚙

How It Works

The system runs a warmup cycle automatically on a schedule. Here is what happens during each cycle:

1

A sender account is picked at random from your warmup accounts pool and sends an email to another account in the pool.

2

After a random delay of 5 to 30 minutes, the receiving account automatically sends a reply back — simulating a real human conversation.

3

Both the send and the reply are logged in the Warmup Log. Gmail registers two-way engagement and raises the sender reputation score over time.

4

You can also upload custom conversation JSON files with specific email chains that will be sent on a timed schedule.

🔥

Warmup Accounts

Warmup accounts are Gmail addresses that exist purely to send and receive warmup traffic. They are separate from your campaign sending accounts. Think of them as a pool of accounts that talk to each other to build domain reputation.

▸

Minimum 2 accounts are required to run warmup. The more accounts in the pool, the more natural the activity looks to Gmail.

▸

App passwords are required, not your regular Gmail password. Go to Google Account → Security → 2-Step Verification → App passwords to generate one for each account.

▸

Recommended daily volume is 5 to 10 warmup emails per account per day. Starting too high too soon can trigger spam filters instead of avoiding them.

▸

Use the Active column on the Warmup Accounts page to pause specific accounts without deleting them from the pool.

💬

Conversations

Conversation files are JSON files that define a scripted email exchange between two or more of your warmup accounts. They give you full control over the content and timing of warmup emails, making the activity look more human.

▸

Generate with ChatGPT: Ask ChatGPT to write a realistic back-and-forth email conversation between two colleagues discussing a project. Paste the output into the JSON format with from, to, subject, body, and send_after_minutes fields.

▸

conversation_id field: Add a "conversation_id": "your-label" key at the top of the JSON to tag all emails from this file so you can filter them in the Warmup Log.

▸

After upload: The system schedules each email based on its send_after_minutes value and sends it using the matching warmup account credentials. The file is recorded in the Conversations history table.

// Example conversation JSON structure
{
  "conversation_id": "week1-batch1",
  "emails": [
    { "from": "acc1@gmail.com", "to": "acc2@gmail.com",
      "subject": "Following up", "body": "Hey, just checking in...",
      "send_after_minutes": 0 },
    { "from": "acc2@gmail.com", "to": "acc1@gmail.com",
      "subject": "Re: Following up", "body": "Thanks, got it!",
      "send_after_minutes": 15 }
  ]
}
📅

Warmup Timeline

A 14-day warmup period is the minimum before starting real cold outreach. Follow this ramp to avoid triggering spam filters:

Days 1 – 7
Send 5 warmup emails per account per day. Keep conversation_id labels consistent. Let replies come through naturally.
Days 8 – 14
Increase to 10 warmup emails per account per day. You should start seeing the domain in Google Postmaster Tools with a good reputation signal.
Day 15+
Start your real campaigns. Continue warmup in parallel at a maintenance volume of 5 emails per account per day to keep the reputation signal active.

Note: Domains with poor SPF / DKIM / DMARC records may need a longer warmup period. Set up DNS authentication records before starting warmup for best results.

📋

Warmup Log

The Warmup Log records every email sent and received as part of the warmup system — both automatic cycles and conversation file emails.

▸

Replied column: A Yes badge means the email was an auto-reply from the receiving account. Seeing replies is a good sign — it means two-way engagement is being recorded by Gmail.

▸

Filter by Conversation: Use the dropdown to isolate emails from a specific conversation file. Auto-generated warmup emails appear under the auto label.

▸

Date range filter: Use the From and To date pickers to narrow the log to a specific period. Filtering is instant and happens in the browser with no page reload.

▸

Reset Log: Clears all warmup log entries permanently. Do this at the start of a fresh warmup cycle or after migrating accounts. Warmup accounts themselves are not affected — only the log records are deleted.

Campaign Manual
📣

What is a Campaign?

A campaign is an automated cold email sequence sent on behalf of a client to a list of leads. You define the emails (called steps), the sending schedule, and the sending account. The system then works through each lead automatically — sending step 1, waiting the configured delay, sending step 2, and so on — until the lead replies, unsubscribes, or finishes the sequence. Every campaign runs independently, so you can run multiple clients at the same time from different sending accounts.

⚙

Sending Accounts

A sending account is the Gmail address that campaign emails are sent from. This should always be a custom domain address, not a personal Gmail.

▸

Use a custom domain: Personal Gmail addresses (@gmail.com) have strict sending limits and are immediately flagged as spam in cold outreach. Use a domain you own — for example outreach@clientdomain.com — routed through Gmail via Google Workspace.

▸

App passwords: Go to the Google Account for the sending address → Security → 2-Step Verification → App passwords. Generate a password for "Mail" and paste it into the App Password field here. Do not use the regular account password.

▸

Status: Set the account to ready before assigning it to a campaign. Accounts marked warming or paused will not send.

▸

Daily limit: Controls the maximum number of emails this account sends per day across all campaigns. Start at 20–30 and raise it gradually after the domain is warmed up.

🗂

Campaign Settings

Each campaign has settings that control when and how fast emails go out. These apply to all leads in the campaign.

Daily Limit
Maximum emails sent per day for this campaign. Shared with the account-level daily limit — whichever is lower wins. Recommended: 20–40 for a warmed domain.
Send Days
Which days of the week emails go out. Default is Monday to Friday. Avoid weekends for B2B outreach — reply rates drop significantly on Sat and Sun.
Send Hours
The hour window (24h format) in which the scheduler is allowed to send. For example 9 to 17 means emails only go out between 9 AM and 5 PM. Best times for B2B are 8–10 AM and 1–3 PM in the recipient's timezone.
Status
Draft — not sending yet. Active — scheduler picks up leads and sends. Paused — sending stops but leads are preserved. Completed — archived, no further sends.
🪜

Sequence Steps

Steps are the individual emails in a campaign sequence. Each lead receives them in order, with a configurable delay between each one. The scheduler only moves a lead to the next step if they have not replied or unsubscribed.

▸

Delay days: How many days after the previous step before this email sends. Step 1 always sends immediately on the lead's first eligible send day. A delay of 3 on step 2 means it sends 3 days after step 1.

▸

Variables: Use {{first_name}}, {{company}}, and {{personalized_line}} anywhere in the subject or body. These are replaced with the lead's actual data when the email is sent.

▸

Preview and test: Use the Preview button on each step to see the email rendered with sample data. Use Send Test to fire a real email to your own inbox before launching.

Recommended 3-step sequence

Step 1
Day 0. Short, personalised intro. One clear ask — a 15-minute call or a yes/no question. Under 100 words.
Step 2
Day 3. Brief follow-up referencing the first email. Add a different angle or a relevant case study. Under 80 words.
Step 3
Day 7. Final breakup email. Keep it light — "no worries if now isn't the right time." Leaves the door open without burning the relationship.
👥

Leads

Leads are the contacts a campaign will email. Each lead belongs to one campaign and progresses through the sequence independently.

▸

Import via CSV: Click Import on the Leads page and paste a CSV with headers first_name, last_name, email, company, website, personalized_line. The system deduplicates by email — re-importing the same address does nothing.

▸

Status column: Active — in sequence, emails scheduled. Replied — a reply was detected, sequence stopped automatically. Finished — completed all steps with no reply. Bounced — email delivery failed. Unsubscribed — opted out.

▸

When a lead replies: The system detects the reply via IMAP, marks the lead as Replied, stops all further emails to them, and pushes the contact to HubSpot if the integration is configured.

▸

Reset: Use the Reset button on a lead to send them back to step 1. Useful if you want to re-run a sequence after a long pause or a product update.

🔄

Campaign Flow

Here is the full lifecycle of a lead from import to HubSpot:

1
Add a sending account and set its status to ready.
2
Create a campaign, assign the account, set daily limit and send schedule.
3
Add sequence steps. Preview and test each one before going live.
4
Import leads via CSV and assign them to the campaign.
5
Set the campaign status to active. The scheduler picks up leads on the next run and starts sending step 1.
6
The system checks for replies every hour via IMAP. Leads that reply are stopped and flagged automatically.
7
Replied leads are pushed to HubSpot as contacts. The client sees them appear in their CRM in real time.
🔗

HubSpot Integration

When a lead replies, the system automatically creates or updates a contact in HubSpot so the client can follow up directly from their CRM.

▸

When a reply is detected: The lead's email, first name, last name, and company are sent to HubSpot via the Contacts API. If a contact with that email already exists it is updated rather than duplicated.

▸

Setting up the token: Go to Settings in the sidebar. Paste a HubSpot Private App token with contacts write scope. The token is stored only in your browser's localStorage — it never touches the server or database.

▸

Giving client access: In HubSpot go to Settings → Users and Teams → Invite User. Add the client's email and assign them the Sales or Viewer role. They will see new contacts appear as replies come in — no access to this system is needed on their end.

▸

If no token is set: The system still sends emails and detects replies normally. HubSpot push is simply skipped. You can add the token at any time and it will apply to all future reply events.
